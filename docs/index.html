01 · How it works

From a posting to a reply, in one place

Every two hours the console looks for new jobs, puts them through your rules and keeps what you decide. You apply; it keeps the record.

01

Find

About a hundred official career sites (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Comeet, Workday) and the alerts LinkedIn, AllJobs and Drushim send to your Gmail.

02

Filter

Your search terms, your regions of Israel, your level. A job you already know never comes back as new.

03

Track

One click puts a job in My Pipeline, with its link and history. Lead roles and engineer roles apart.

04

Hear back

"We got your application" marks it as sent; "we decided not to move forward" closes it. Your own later update always wins.

02 · Filters

Nothing is hidden without a reason

Every rule that holds a job back says how many jobs it holds back, and can be switched off to look.

  • a
    Your words, your regionsExcluded title words, the parts of Israel that are too far for you, single places too.
  • b
    Your level firstLead, manager and head roles at the top; a job asking one or two years of experience is held back as junior.
  • c
    The employer's own page firstEach job links to the employer's posting or career site before LinkedIn and job boards, with recruiters to look for.

04 · Privacy

Your search stays yours

The console is one folder on your computer. Nothing to sign up for, nobody to trust with your history.

Where your data is
~/CareerConsole/data: the database, your settings, your mailbox's app password. Never uploaded, never part of the code you download.
What it talks to
The career sites it checks and your own mailbox, read-only: nothing is marked, moved or deleted. It listens only on your computer (127.0.0.1).
LinkedIn
Never scraped. LinkedIn jobs come only from the alert e-mails LinkedIn sends you.
What it costs
Nothing. No paid services, no API keys, no AI.
Backups
uv run career-console backup writes one self-contained file; restore brings it back.
05 · First steps

Ten minutes to a working search

After the install the console opens in your browser.

  1. 01

    Search settings

    Your search terms, excluded words, your level and the regions too far for you. The defaults are for QA and test automation, lead and senior roles: change them to your field.

  2. 02

    Connect your Gmail

    Search settings → Job-alert e-mails. Gmail needs an app password, not your normal one: turn on 2-Step Verification, then create one at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords.

  3. 03

    Add your applications so far

    "Add an application" on My Pipeline: a link and a few words, or dictate them. The search never shows them to you as new.

  4. 04

    Let it search by itself

    Every two hours from 8:00 to 22:00: cd ~/CareerConsole && uv run career-console schedule --install. Or press Search Jobs Now on your desktop.

Install

One line in Terminal, then one icon

Run the same line again whenever you want the newest version. Your data stays as it is.
